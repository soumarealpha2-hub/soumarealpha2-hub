# Echo Rotating Lamp

## Context

The Echo Rotating Lamp was an interactive physical-product project developed in an NYU engineering and VIP environment. The prototype combined rotational movement with sound-responsive behavior to create a more engaging and adjustable lighting experience.

## The challenge

The team needed to move beyond an idea and deliver a working prototype that was stable, usable, and ready for a live presentation. That required coordinating mechanical movement, basic electronics, testing, documentation, and team deadlines as one connected product-development process.

## My role

I served as the project manager and tester. I organized milestones and deliverables, kept the team aligned around presentation readiness, and tested the lamp's movement, stability, sound response, and overall usability.

## Our approach

1. Defined the core user experience: a lamp that could rotate and react to sound.
2. Divided the build into clear design, hardware, assembly, and testing milestones.
3. Coordinated progress across the team and surfaced issues early enough to protect the delivery schedule.
4. Tested the prototype repeatedly for reliable movement, physical stability, and ease of use.
5. Refined both the product and the presentation based on what the tests revealed.

## Outcome

- Delivered a working lamp prototype that rotated and responded to sound
- Completed the required project deliverables and presentation materials
- Presented the prototype to NYU professors, advisors, and VIP leadership
- Demonstrated how project management and hands-on testing can turn a technical concept into a usable physical product

## What I learned

Physical product development makes every dependency visible. A strong concept is only the beginning. The mechanism, electronics, usability, testing process, and team execution all have to work together. This project strengthened my ability to translate between technical work and the operational decisions needed to deliver it.
