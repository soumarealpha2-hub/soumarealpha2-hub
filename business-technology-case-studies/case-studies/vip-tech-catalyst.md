# VIP Tech Catalyst Program Operations

## Context

NYU's Vertically Integrated Projects environment brings students with different technical backgrounds together around faculty-guided engineering work. Teams needed shared direction on tools, milestones, and sprint execution.

## Problem

With more than 30 students contributing across projects, technical decisions and delivery expectations could become fragmented. The program needed a clearer link between technology choices, team ownership, and weekly execution.

## My role

As a Tech Catalyst, I supported technology-stack evaluation, agile sprint planning, cross-team alignment, and roadmap development. I also recruited and coordinated a team working on an optics-sensor project, managed deadlines, reported progress to the faculty advisor, and helped present the work at a showcase.

## Approach

1. Compared tools against project needs and team capability.
2. Broke roadmaps into sprint-sized commitments.
3. Clarified ownership and surfaced blockers early.
4. Facilitated alignment across students working at different levels of technical depth.
5. Connected project status to faculty expectations and showcase readiness.

## Results

- Supported alignment across a **30+ student** technical program
- Co-authored roadmaps that connected technology evaluation to sprint execution
- Recruited and coordinated an optics-sensor project team
- Managed milestones and reported progress to the faculty advisor
- Helped bring the project through a public showcase presentation

## What I learned

Technical program leadership is translation work. It requires enough technical fluency to understand tradeoffs and enough operational discipline to help people turn those decisions into delivered work.
